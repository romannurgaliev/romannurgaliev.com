Brighty

Oct 2025–Apr 2026

Trading terminal and B2B onboarding

  • Switzerland, remote
  • ~20-person company
  • One frontend engineer

Overview

I was the only designer on two products at Brighty, a Swiss crypto fintech of about 20 people: a perps and spot trading terminal with up to 50x leverage, and Brighty Business, the product for business accounts.

Trading Terminal

In 2025 Brighty launched the terminal to bring a new audience to its core products, on the wave of interest in perps trading. With leverage up to 50x, a small price move can liquidate a position.

  • Designed the terminal and took it through public release
  • Worked with one frontend engineer through specs and design tokens, and reviewed the build before launch
  • Built the design system and the token set used in handoff
  • Designed a cross-margin risk simulator together with the product owner

Cross-Margin Risk Simulator

In cross margin, all positions share one pool of collateral. Traders tend to read each liquidation price as fixed, but a drop in one asset eats into the shared equity and pulls every other liquidation price closer to the market. The simulator lets a trader try a scenario before it happens.

How the simulator works
  • Each open position is a row with a price slider from −50% to +50%, a liquidation marker that moves with the scenario, a danger zone, and the position’s stop loss and take profit
  • Presets for common shocks: the whole market down 30%, BTC up 15%, the market up 10%, and a reset to market prices
  • A cross-margin ratio bar colored by risk level (low, medium, high), with maintenance margin, effective leverage, the market move to liquidation, and withdrawable funds
  • Two ways in: from the risk tooltip in the terminal, and from the order form, to see how a new order affects open positions before placing it

The product owner and I worked out the risk model together; I designed the interaction and the interface and took the simulator to a complete prototype.

Brighty Business

Brighty Business gives companies multi-currency accounts, cards, and transfers. Before a company can use its account, it has to pass KYB, and business onboarding is where most of the friction was.

  • Redesigned KYB onboarding and took the proposal through review with engineering and compliance
  • Benchmarked Brighty Business against six B2B finance products (Slash, Finom, Brex, Ramp, Deel, Papaya) on approvals, roles, and audit

KYB Onboarding

Business onboarding was long and had no point to pause. Companies entered the same data more than once and had to download, sign, and re-upload provider documents.

I mapped every field and document in the flow, marked each one as ask, pre-fill, reuse, or remove, and proposed three changes:

  • A two-stage flow: a short set of fields before the home page, the rest after the first login, with more features unlocking as the company provides more data
  • Pre-fill for data the company has already given, such as offering the legal address as the operational one
  • Provider documents prepared from data already collected, instead of download, sign, and re-upload

Outcome

The terminal shipped to public release, and the risk simulator reached a complete prototype. For Brighty Business, I delivered a field map and a two-stage onboarding proposal.

A note on the simulator: in October 2026 I rebuilt the mockups from work-in-progress exports, recalculated every number with Hyperliquid’s published liquidation formulas, and built the live prototype on this page in Claude Code. The rebuild settled a few open questions: risk levels by distance to liquidation, the market move to liquidation, and stops that fire before or after liquidation. It also adds what the original didn’t have: before-and-after values, suggested fixes, and two real crashes replayed from Hyperliquid prices.
